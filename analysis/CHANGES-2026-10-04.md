## 1. Census

### 1.1 Task-level correlation rho (new)

Definition (`estimate_rho.py` docstring): for entries i, j with per-task success rates p_i(t), p_j(t), rho = [mean_t p_i p_j - P_i P_j] / sqrt(P_i(1-P_i) P_j(1-P_j)). For two single-run entries this is exactly the Pearson (phi) correlation of the per-task binary outcomes. For entries averaging k > 1 runs (Terminal-Bench 1.0 and 2.0) it is the expected phi between one run of each, which is the form that matches the bound's single-run variance P(1-P); Pearson of the per-task rates, which is higher, is recorded in `rho_by_board.csv`. Per board: median and IQR over adjacent pairs in census order. Seven boards have per-task data locally (the four SWE-bench splits, Terminal-Bench 1.0 and 2.0, TheAgentCompany); the other 15 publish aggregates only and use the pooled median.

## 2. RQ3 re-analysis (Terminal-Bench 2.0)

Run-to-run scheme corrected for the (r-1)/r bias of resampling r runs with replacement. Choice: each task's resampled success rate is rescaled about its observed rate by sqrt(N/(N-1)) (N = runs of that task; N = 5, 6 or 10 here), so the resample variance is phat(1-phat)/(N-1), the unbiased estimate of p(1-p)/N. For equal N this equals scaling the bootstrap SD by sqrt(N/(N-1)) (1.118 at N = 5); per-task scaling handles mixed N exactly. Same seeds and replicate counts; flip probabilities are recomputed from the corrected replicates. A closed-form check (`analytic_run_sd`) is stored as `run_sd_analytic` in `tb2_bootstrap.csv`.

## 3. Within-task variance v, run-noise share f and the detectability bound

v (unbiased mean within-task variance, median over the 72 submissions) is unchanged at 0.0753 (min 0.0000, max 0.2022; plug-in max 0.1618): the paper's v already carried the r/(r-1) correction, so the correction changes the bootstrap, not v. New: f = v/(P(1-P)) has median 0.3689 (IQR 0.325 to 0.434; range 0.000 to 0.844; the 0.000 is the flagged submission) over 72 submissions; without the flagged submission median 0.3691 (IQR 0.326 to 0.434; range 0.140 to 0.844); corr(v, P) = -0.334.

Detectability bound, defined exactly: Delta_min = z sqrt(2 v/(n k)), the smallest observed difference between two k-run means that is significant at 95% (two-sided z = 1.96); it is 50% power at a true difference equal to Delta_min, and no power claim is made beyond that. `results.md`, section 'Run-noise share f and the detectability bound', holds the same numbers.

| case | OLD = NEW: z = 1.96, v = 0.0753 | z = 1.645 (one-sided 5%) | NEW: v = f P(1-P), f = 0.369, P = 0.2 | P = 0.5 | P = 0.8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| n = 100, k = 1 (worked bound, Section 4) | 7.61 | 6.38 | 6.73 | 8.42 | 6.73 |
| Terminal-Bench 2.0 design, n = 89, k = 5 | 3.61 | 3.03 | 3.19 | 3.99 | 3.19 |
| ReliabilityBench check, n = 20, k = 2 | 12.02 | 10.09 | 10.65 | 13.31 | 10.65 |

The worked bound of 7.6 pp (n = 100, k = 1, v = 0.0753) does not change under the correction. If v is transported as a fraction f of P(1-P) instead of as an absolute value, the same benchmark gives 8.42 pp at P = 0.5 and 6.73 pp at P = 0.2 or 0.8. At the design of Terminal-Bench 2.0 the analytic bound stays 3.61 pp; the bootstrap's largest unstable gap moved from 2.92 to 3.60 pp, against 3.03 pp for the analytic bound under the one-sided z of the 5% rule. The bootstrap value is the largest unstable gap over 2,556 pairs and the analytic value a single threshold at the median v, so their closeness is not a validation of either.
